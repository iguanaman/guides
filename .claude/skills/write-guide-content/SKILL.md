# Writing Guide Content

Collate, don't summarize away. Every distinct claim/insight/exception a `.research/*.md` source contains should land in the guide somewhere — pick where it fits and how to phrase it, but don't drop items because they seem minor, redundant-sounding, or don't fit a tidy narrative. Rolling several sources' distinct points into one vaguer sentence is a failure even if the sentence is well-written.
One guide voice, no source meta — write as a game guide a player reads, never "one analysis says / a reviewer counters" or references to models/threads/valuations. Sources disagree → resolve into conditional advice keeping both substances ("strong when X because…, weak when Y because…"), never attribute or average into mush.
Teach transferable insight, not formulas — WHY a play/build/approach is strong, WHEN it applies, tradeoffs and situational exceptions. Reader should come away able to reason about novel situations, not just follow a script.
Don't reproduce step-by-step walkthroughs (turn-by-turn openers, exact build orders, "do A then B then C") — teach the principle behind them: why this opener, what conditions favor it, how to adapt when conditions differ.
Depth over brevity — go deep on strategy and edge cases; surface-level summaries defeat the purpose. Prefer thorough over concise for guide content itself (this rule doesn't apply to CLAUDE.md/skill files).
Short paragraphs — split at each idea shift; a paragraph over ~5 sentences needs splitting.
End each card/section with a short recap paragraph of the key points.
Solo guide: solo is the default perspective, never a footnote/"solo note" — fold solo implications into the main advice.
Not a reference/rulebook mirror — skip stats, card text, mechanics-as-written, or anything findable in-game or in the manual. Cover oft-repeated community strategy/advice AND notable insight from individual sources AND general tips/tricks (secondary focus).
